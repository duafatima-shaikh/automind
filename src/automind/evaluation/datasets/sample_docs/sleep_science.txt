Sleep Science Notes

Adults need between 7 and 9 hours of sleep each night, while teenagers need between 8 and 10 hours. Sleep happens in cycles that last about 90 minutes. Each cycle moves through light sleep, deep sleep, and rapid eye movement sleep, often called REM sleep.

Deep sleep is most common in the first half of the night and helps the body repair muscles and strengthen the immune system. REM sleep is more common toward morning and is linked to dreaming and memory storage. Waking up in the middle of a deep sleep stage often causes grogginess.

Caffeine blocks a brain chemical called adenosine, which builds up during the day and creates the feeling of tiredness. The effects of caffeine last for about 5 to 6 hours, so experts suggest avoiding coffee after early afternoon. Alcohol may help a person fall asleep faster but reduces REM sleep and causes more waking during the night.

The body clock, known as the circadian rhythm, responds strongly to light. Bright morning light helps set the clock, while bright screens late at night can delay the release of melatonin, a hormone that signals bedtime. A cool bedroom, around 18 degrees Celsius, helps most people sleep better.

Keeping the same wake up time every day, even on weekends, is one of the most effective habits for better sleep. A short nap of 20 minutes can improve alertness without causing deep sleep grogginess, but napping late in the day can make it harder to fall asleep at night.
