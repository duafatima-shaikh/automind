Coffee Brewing Guide

The two most important factors in brewing coffee are the ratio of coffee to water and the temperature of the water. A common starting ratio is 1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water. The ideal water temperature is between 90 and 96 degrees Celsius, just below boiling.

Grind size changes how fast water pulls flavor out of the coffee. A fine grind extracts quickly and can taste bitter if brewed too long. A coarse grind extracts slowly and can taste weak and sour. French press brewing uses a coarse grind, while espresso uses a very fine grind.

A pour over method such as the V60 usually takes about 3 minutes in total. The brewer first pours a small amount of water to let the coffee bloom for 30 seconds, which releases trapped carbon dioxide. After the bloom, the rest of the water is poured in slow circles.

A French press steeps for 4 minutes before the plunger is pressed down. Leaving coffee in the press after brewing keeps extraction going and makes the drink bitter, so it should be poured out right away.

Fresh beans give the best flavor. Coffee tastes best within about 4 weeks after the roast date, and beans should be stored in an airtight container away from light and heat. Grinding just before brewing keeps the aroma from fading.
